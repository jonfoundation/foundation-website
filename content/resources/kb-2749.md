<p>Completing an accessory dwelling unit does not end the owner’s responsibilities. Insurance coverage, routine maintenance, warranty administration and organized records all help protect the property and reduce confusion when repairs, claims, refinancing or a future sale occur. These systems should be planned before occupancy—not assembled only after a problem develops.</p>
<section id="review-insurance-before-construction-and-again-before-occupancy"><h2>Review Insurance Before Construction and Again Before Occupancy</h2>
<p>Tell the property insurer about the proposed ADU before work begins.
Construction can change the property’s replacement value, liability exposure and occupancy. Depending on the project and existing coverage,
the owner, contractor or lender may need particular construction-period policies, endorsements or certificates.</p>
<p>Before the ADU is occupied, review the completed use with an insurance professional. Coverage needs may differ when the unit will house family, serve as a long-term rental, remain vacant for periods of time or be used in another lawful way. Do not assume the existing homeowner policy automatically covers the new structure, its contents,
rental income or all activities associated with it.</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Confirm the ADU is included in the insured property description.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Update the estimated replacement cost and report major systems or features.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Discuss property, liability, loss-of-use and loss-of-rents coverage as applicable.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Ask how shared utilities, detached structures and accessory spaces are treated.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Confirm whether a tenant should maintain renters insurance.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Keep written confirmation of coverage decisions and policy changes.</p>
</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section id="create-a-permanent-adu-record"><h2>Create a Permanent ADU Record</h2>
<p>A complete project record is valuable long after the contractor leaves. It helps owners identify concealed conditions, order replacement parts, demonstrate permitted completion and provide accurate information to insurers, lenders, buyers and service professionals.</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Approved plans, specifications and revisions</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Permits, inspection records and final occupancy documentation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Contracts, change orders, invoices and proof of payment</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Engineering reports, surveys and geotechnical information</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Product submittals, finish schedules and paint colors</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Equipment model and serial numbers</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Warranties, registrations and maintenance requirements</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Utility approvals, meter information and service records</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Photographs of framing, waterproofing and concealed utilities before close-in</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Contractor, subcontractor, designer and consultant contact information</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Keep important records in a backed-up digital folder with clear filenames. Retain original paper documents when signatures, seals or agency records make them important.</p>
</section>
<section id="understand-what-each-warranty-covers"><h2>Understand What Each Warranty Covers</h2>
<p>An ADU may have several overlapping warranties: the contractor’s workmanship warranty, manufacturer warranties for products and equipment, and warranties from specialty installers. These warranties may have different durations, exclusions, notice requirements and maintenance conditions.</p>
<p>Create a summary showing the covered item, warranty provider, start date, expiration date, contact method, required documentation and owner maintenance obligations. Register products when required and save confirmation emails or registration numbers.</p>
<p>A warranty is not a substitute for maintenance. Failure to clean,
service, protect or operate an item as instructed can shorten its life and may affect coverage.</p>
</section>
<section id="build-a-maintenance-calendar"><h2>Build a Maintenance Calendar</h2>
<p>The ADU should be added to the property’s recurring maintenance plan.
Manufacturer instructions, local climate, surrounding vegetation,
occupancy and equipment type will affect the schedule.</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Replace or clean heating and cooling filters.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Service heating, cooling and water-heating equipment as recommended.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Test smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms and replace batteries or devices as required.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Clean kitchen and bathroom exhaust systems.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Inspect sealants, grout, flashing and other water-management details.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Clear roof drainage, site drains and exterior drainage paths.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Look for leaks beneath sinks, at supply connections and near appliances.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Exercise accessible water shutoff valves when appropriate.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Inspect exterior finishes, windows, doors and weatherstripping.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Follow maintenance instructions for solar, battery, fire-sprinkler or other specialty systems.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Some tasks should be performed only by licensed or otherwise qualified professionals. The maintenance file should identify who performs each task and when it was completed.</p>
</section>
<section id="watch-for-water-and-moisture-problems"><h2>Watch for Water and Moisture Problems</h2>
<p>Water-related damage can become expensive before it is obvious. Pay attention to staining, musty odors, soft finishes, recurring condensation, unusually high water bills and slow drainage. Exterior grading, irrigation and roof drainage should continue to direct water away from the building as intended.</p>
<p>If the ADU is rented or occupied by a family member, explain how to report leaks and moisture promptly. A simple written procedure can prevent a small maintenance issue from becoming a larger property and indoor-air-quality problem.</p>
</section>
<section id="keep-utility-and-equipment-information-accessible"><h2>Keep Utility and Equipment Information Accessible</h2>
<p>Label electrical panels, breakers, water shutoffs, gas valves,
cleanouts, filters and major equipment. Store operating instructions where the owner and authorized occupants can find them without searching through construction records.</p>
<p>Maintain a diagram or photo record of underground utilities and concealed lines. This is especially helpful before future landscaping,
fencing, solar, additions or repairs.</p>
</section>
<section id="document-repairs-and-changes"><h2>Document Repairs and Changes</h2>
<p>Keep a dated record of maintenance, repairs, replacements and improvements. Include the problem observed, service provider, work performed, cost, photos, receipts and any new warranty information.</p>
<p>Future modifications may require permits or professional design.
Documenting changes helps preserve an accurate history of the ADU and reduces the risk that later work damages concealed utilities,
waterproofing or structural components.</p>
</section>
<section id="update-records-when-occupancy-or-use-changes"><h2>Update Records When Occupancy or Use Changes</h2>
<p>An ADU may serve different purposes over its life. A family unit may later become a rental, or a rental may return to personal use. When occupancy changes, review insurance, lease or household documents,
utility billing, access procedures, maintenance responsibilities and any applicable local requirements.</p>
<p>If the property is refinanced, transferred or sold, organized ADU records can help the owner respond to questions about permits,
construction cost, warranties, rental history and ongoing obligations.</p>
</section>
<section id="prepare-for-emergencies-and-claims"><h2>Prepare for Emergencies and Claims</h2>
<p>Keep emergency contacts, policy information, utility shutoff instructions and property access details available. Photograph the completed ADU and major installed equipment. If damage occurs, take reasonable steps to protect people and prevent further loss, then follow the insurer’s reporting instructions.</p>
<p>Do not discard damaged materials or authorize major non-emergency repairs before understanding documentation and inspection requirements.
Claim procedures vary, so obtain guidance from the insurer and appropriate professionals.</p>
</section>
<section id="foundation-tip"><h2>Foundation Tip</h2>
<p>Create the ADU operations folder during closeout, not months later.
Ask for manuals, warranties, finish information, model numbers,
inspection records and concealed-condition photos before final payment,
while the project team can still retrieve missing information.</p>
</section>
<section id="how-foundation-can-help"><h2>How Foundation Can Help</h2>
<p>Foundation can help homeowners organize closeout and ownership questions before important information is lost. A Foundation Strategy Session can help identify records to request, maintenance responsibilities to clarify and questions to raise with the contractor,
insurer and other qualified professionals. The Foundation Final Walk™
can also help document visible punch items, incomplete work, warranty questions and other closeout items to discuss with the contractor before final payment; it does not replace required inspections, professional testing or agency approval.</p>
</section>
