<p>An ADU scope is more than a floor plan and a construction price.</p>
<p>The complete project may include feasibility work, surveys, design, engineering, permits, demolition, utility upgrades, site access, drainage, landscaping, interior finishes, appliances, inspections and closeout. Some items may be included in a designer’s or builder’s proposal. Others may remain with the homeowner or a separate consultant.</p>
<p>If the scope is not defined clearly, two proposals can appear to describe the same ADU while covering very different projects.</p>
<p>A useful scope should explain what will be built, what work supports it, who is responsible for each part, which assumptions are being used and what remains unknown.</p>
<section id="begin-with-the-adus-purpose"><h2>Begin With the ADU’s Purpose</h2>
<p>Scope decisions should follow the reason for building the ADU.</p>
<p>Clarify:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Who is expected to use the ADU</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Whether the use is family, rental, guest, work or long-term flexibility</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>The desired level of independence and privacy</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>The number of occupants and bedrooms</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Accessibility or aging-in-place priorities</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Expected storage, laundry and outdoor-space needs</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Whether the use may change over time</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>A project intended for an older family member may require a different entrance, bathroom, route from parking and mechanical strategy than a compact rental unit.</p>
</section>
<section id="define-the-adu-type-before-defining-the-work"><h2>Define the ADU Type Before Defining the Work</h2>
<p>The basic project type changes the scope.</p>
<p>Common paths include:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>A new detached ADU</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>An attached ADU addition</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>A garage or accessory-structure conversion</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Conversion of space within the primary home</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>A JADU</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>A prefabricated, modular or manufactured unit</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>An ADU created on a multifamily property</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>A project using a local affordable- or bonus-ADU program</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Each path creates different structural, utility, access, permitting and integration work. Do not compare prices until the project type is consistent.</p>
</section>
<section id="separate-the-building-scope-from-the-project-scope"><h2>Separate the Building Scope From the Project Scope</h2>
<p>The building scope describes the physical ADU. The project scope includes everything required to plan, approve, deliver and complete it.</p>
<p>A building-only description might identify square footage, rooms and finishes.</p>
<p>A complete project scope should also address:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Professional and technical services</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Agency coordination and fees</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Demolition and temporary conditions</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Utility investigation and connections</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Site work and drainage</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Construction access and protection</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Testing, inspections and closeout</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Owner-furnished work and products</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Known exclusions and unresolved conditions</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>This distinction is especially important when a prefab provider prices the unit but a separate contractor must complete foundations, trenching, utility connections, craning and finish work.</p>
</section>
<section id="document-existing-conditions"><h2>Document Existing Conditions</h2>
<p>A reliable scope starts with the property as it exists, not only the proposed plan.</p>
<p>Document:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Lot dimensions and property lines</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Topography and drainage patterns</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Existing buildings and accessory structures</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Driveways, gates, walls, fences and exterior stairs</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Trees, landscaping and irrigation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Water, sewer, gas, electrical and communications services</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Utility meters, panels, cleanouts and connection points</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Easements and recorded restrictions</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Fire access and mapped hazard conditions</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Visible damage, settlement or unpermitted work</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Unknown conditions should be identified as unknowns rather than converted into optimistic assumptions.</p>
</section>
<section id="professional-and-technical-services"><h2>Professional and Technical Services</h2>
<p>Define which planning and design services are included and who will provide them.</p>
<p>Potential services include:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Preliminary feasibility review</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Boundary or topographic survey</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Architectural or building design</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Structural engineering</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Civil engineering and drainage design</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Geotechnical investigation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Energy-code documentation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Fire-protection design</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Landscape or brush-management plans</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Utility coordination</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Permit preparation and response to plan-check comments</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Construction administration or site observation</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not assume that “plans included” means every consultant, calculation, revision or agency response is included.</p>
</section>
<section id="agency-and-permit-scope"><h2>Agency and Permit Scope</h2>
<p>The scope should identify the anticipated approvals and who is responsible for obtaining them.</p>
<p>Depending on the property and project, this may include:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Building permit</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Planning or zoning review</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Coastal development approval</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Grading or right-of-way permits</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Fire-sprinkler or other deferred permits</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Utility-provider applications</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Address assignment</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>School, impact, connection or capacity fees</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Special inspections</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Final inspections and occupancy approval</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Clarify whether permit fees are included, estimated, passed through at cost or excluded.</p>
</section>
<section id="demolition-and-site-preparation"><h2>Demolition and Site Preparation</h2>
<p>Early work is often described too broadly.</p>
<p>A complete demolition and preparation scope may need to address:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Removal of an existing garage, shed or paving</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Selective demolition for an attached or converted ADU</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Hazardous-material testing and abatement</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Tree or landscape removal</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Utility disconnection or relocation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Protection of the occupied home</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Temporary fencing and dust control</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Hauling, disposal and recycling</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Compaction or preparation of the building pad</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Restoration of areas disturbed by construction</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Identify what is known, what is based on visual inspection and what can only be confirmed after demolition.</p>
</section>
<section id="foundations-structure-and-building-envelope"><h2>Foundations, Structure and Building Envelope</h2>
<p>Define the structural system and the work required to create a complete weather-resistant building.</p>
<p>The scope may include:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Excavation and foundations</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Retaining walls or grade beams</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Termite or soil treatment</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Floor, wall and roof framing</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Structural connections to an existing building</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Sheathing and weather barriers</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Roofing and flashing</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Exterior doors and windows</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Siding, stucco or other exterior finishes</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Insulation and air sealing</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Fire-rated assemblies and opening protection</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Gutters and roof drainage</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>For a conversion, the scope should distinguish existing components that will remain from components that must be repaired, reinforced or replaced.</p>
</section>
<section id="interior-construction-and-finishes"><h2>Interior Construction and Finishes</h2>
<p>Interior scope should be specific enough to establish the expected quality level.</p>
<p>Address:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Interior partitions and ceilings</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Drywall and texture</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Interior doors, trim and hardware</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Flooring and baseboards</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Painting</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Cabinetry and countertops</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Closets and built-in storage</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Window coverings</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Accessories such as mirrors, shelving and bath hardware</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Acoustic treatments between the ADU and primary home</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Terms such as “standard,” “builder grade” and “allowance” should be defined rather than left open to interpretation.</p>
</section>
<section id="kitchen-scope"><h2>Kitchen Scope</h2>
<p>An ADU kitchen involves more than cabinets and appliances.</p>
<p>Define:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Layout and cabinet configuration</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Cabinet material and hardware</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Countertop material and edge</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Backsplash</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Sink, faucet and disposal</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Cooking equipment and ventilation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Refrigerator, dishwasher and microwave</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Lighting and electrical outlets</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Appliance delivery, installation and utility connections</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Whether appliances are contractor-furnished or owner-furnished</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>For a JADU, confirm that the kitchen scope follows the applicable JADU requirements rather than a full-ADU assumption.</p>
</section>
<section id="bathroom-scope"><h2>Bathroom Scope</h2>
<p>Bathroom scope should identify both visible finishes and concealed waterproofing work.</p>
<p>Address:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Layout and fixture count</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Tub or shower configuration</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Waterproofing system</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Tile extent and pattern</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Vanity, countertop and sink</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Toilet and plumbing fixtures</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Shower enclosure or curtain provision</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Ventilation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Lighting and electrical</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Accessories, blocking and future grab-bar locations</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Accessibility decisions should be made early because they affect space, structure, fixtures and entry design.</p>
</section>
<section id="plumbing-and-sewer-scope"><h2>Plumbing and Sewer Scope</h2>
<p>Utility connections are a common source of incomplete ADU pricing.</p>
<p>The plumbing scope should clarify:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>The proposed water connection</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Available water pressure and capacity</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Water-meter or service upgrades</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Hot-water system</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Sewer connection point and route</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Pipe size, slope and cleanouts</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Trenching and restoration</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Pumps or special systems, if needed</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Utility-provider fees and inspections</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Existing lines should not be assumed adequate without investigation.</p>
</section>
<section id="electrical-energy-and-low-voltage-scope"><h2>Electrical, Energy and Low-Voltage Scope</h2>
<p>Define how the ADU will be powered and metered.</p>
<p>Potential scope includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Load calculations</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Existing service and panel evaluation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Service, meter or panel upgrades</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>A new ADU subpanel</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Trenching and feeder routes</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Interior lighting and devices</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Exterior lighting</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Solar photovoltaic requirements</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Electric-vehicle or e-bike charging</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Internet, data and security wiring</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Utility applications and coordination</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Clarify whether utility-company work is included, carried as an allowance or excluded.</p>
</section>
<section id="heating-cooling-and-ventilation-scope"><h2>Heating, Cooling and Ventilation Scope</h2>
<p>Mechanical scope should describe the complete comfort and ventilation strategy.</p>
<p>Address:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Heating and cooling equipment</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Equipment size and efficiency</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Indoor and outdoor unit locations</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Condensate routing</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Kitchen exhaust</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Bathroom exhaust</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Fresh-air or whole-building ventilation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Controls and thermostats</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Electrical or structural support for the equipment</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Patching and finish work at existing construction</p>
</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section id="site-work-drainage-and-exterior-improvements"><h2>Site Work, Drainage and Exterior Improvements</h2>
<p>The area around the ADU is part of the project even when it is not part of the building.</p>
<p>Define:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Grading and drainage</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Stormwater measures</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Walkways and steps</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Patios and private outdoor space</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Fences, gates and privacy screening</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Parking and driveway work</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Retaining walls</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Landscaping and irrigation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Exterior lighting</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Trash and recycling storage</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Address signage and mail delivery</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Restoration of yards, paving and planting disturbed by utility trenches</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>A price that ends at the exterior wall may leave a large amount of unfinished site work.</p>
</section>
<section id="fire-access-and-life-safety-scope"><h2>Fire Access and Life-Safety Scope</h2>
<p>Coordinate the scope with the findings from fire-access, separation and sprinkler review.</p>
<p>Potential work includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Required setbacks and fire-rated assemblies</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Emergency access routes</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Address identification</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Defensible space and brush management</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Fire-sprinkler design and installation</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Water-supply or meter upgrades</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Emergency escape and rescue openings</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Construction-phase fire access</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not treat “sprinklers not required” as a complete life-safety scope.</p>
</section>
<section id="construction-logistics-and-protection"><h2>Construction Logistics and Protection</h2>
<p>ADUs are often built behind or beside an occupied home with limited access.</p>
<p>Define responsibility for:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Construction access and staging</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Temporary fencing and security</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Protection of the home, driveway and landscaping</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Temporary utility shutdowns</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Dust, noise and debris control</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Material deliveries and lifting equipment</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Portable toilets and temporary facilities</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Neighbor coordination</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Daily cleanup</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Repair of construction damage</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Restricted access can affect labor, equipment choices, schedule and cost.</p>
</section>
<section id="selections-allowances-and-owner-furnished-items"><h2>Selections, Allowances and Owner-Furnished Items</h2>
<p>Every material and product should have a defined procurement path.</p>
<p>Classify items as:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Fully selected and included</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Included through a stated allowance</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Owner-furnished and contractor-installed</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Owner-furnished and owner-installed</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Excluded</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Not yet determined</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>For allowances, identify what the amount covers. A material-only allowance may not include tax, delivery, installation, accessories or waste.</p>
</section>
<section id="owner-responsibilities"><h2>Owner Responsibilities</h2>
<p>A clear scope identifies what the homeowner must do.</p>
<p>Possible responsibilities include:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Providing surveys or existing records</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Making timely design and material decisions</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Paying agency and utility fees</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Purchasing appliances or fixtures</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Coordinating financing</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Removing personal property</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Providing access to the site</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Maintaining required insurance</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Approving changes and added costs</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Coordinating work performed by separate vendors</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Undefined owner responsibilities often become schedule problems.</p>
</section>
<section id="exclusions-assumptions-and-unknowns"><h2>Exclusions, Assumptions and Unknowns</h2>
<p>A strong scope is transparent about what is not known.</p>
<p>List:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Specific exclusions</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Assumed existing conditions</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Unverified utility capacity</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Potential hazardous materials</p>
</li>
<li>

<section id="a-practical-scope-definition-sequence"><h2>A Practical Scope-Definition Sequence</h2>
<h3 id="define-the-use-and-success-criteria">1. Define the use and success criteria</h3>
<p>Clarify who will use the ADU, how it should function and which outcomes matter most.</p>
<h3 id="confirm-the-project-type-and-preliminary-feasibility">2. Confirm the project type and preliminary feasibility</h3>
<p>Identify the likely ADU configuration and the property conditions that may shape it.</p>
<h3 id="document-existing-conditions-1">3. Document existing conditions</h3>
<p>Gather surveys, records, measurements, utility information and field observations.</p>
<h3 id="break-the-project-into-work-categories">4. Break the project into work categories</h3>
<p>Separate design, permits, demolition, building construction, utilities, site work, logistics and closeout.</p>
<h3 id="assign-responsibility">5. Assign responsibility</h3>
<p>Identify the designer, consultants, contractor, vendors, agencies and owner responsibilities.</p>
<h3 id="state-selections-allowances-and-assumptions">6. State selections, allowances and assumptions</h3>
<p>Define the quality level and mark every unresolved item clearly.</p>
<h3 id="identify-exclusions-and-risks">7. Identify exclusions and risks</h3>
<p>Document what is outside the price and what may change after investigation or agency review.</p>
<h3 id="reconcile-scope-with-the-budget-and-schedule">8. Reconcile scope with the budget and schedule</h3>
<p>Confirm that the project plan includes the complete work, not only the visible building.</p>
<h3 id="use-the-same-scope-structure-for-proposal-comparison">9. Use the same scope structure for proposal comparison</h3>
<p>Require each proposer to address the same categories so differences remain visible.</p>
<h3 id="update-the-scope-as-the-design-develops">10. Update the scope as the design develops</h3>
<p>Scope definition is not a one-time exercise. Revise it as information, selections and approvals become more specific.</p>
</section>
