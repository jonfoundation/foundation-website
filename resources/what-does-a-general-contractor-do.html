General Contractor Responsibilities

What Does a General Contractor Actually Do?

Look beyond trade coordination to understand the planning, scheduling, procurement, communication, quality, safety and risk-management work behind construction.

By Jon Lourash · Foundation Renovation Coaching

Published September 21, 2026

In This Guide

A common misconception is that a general contractor's primary responsibility is hiring subcontractors. While coordinating trades is certainly an important part of the job, it represents only one piece of a much larger role. A good general contractor is responsible for organizing, managing, and guiding the entire construction process from start to finish.

Long before construction begins, a contractor may review plans, develop budgets, identify constructability concerns, recommend material selections, prepare schedules, and coordinate with designers or engineers. Their goal is to transform a set of drawings into a practical plan that can be built safely, efficiently, and within the project's objectives.

Once construction starts, the contractor becomes the central point of coordination. They schedule trades, procure materials, oversee quality, monitor progress, coordinate inspections, manage permits, address unforeseen conditions, and keep communication flowing between everyone involved in the project.

General contractors also manage risk. They verify that work complies with applicable building codes, maintain jobsite safety, resolve conflicts between trades, and adapt the schedule when unexpected conditions arise. Renovations rarely proceed exactly as planned, and one of the contractor's most valuable responsibilities is keeping the project moving despite those challenges.

Throughout construction, the contractor also serves as an information manager. They document decisions, communicate changes, review requests for additional work, monitor costs, and help homeowners understand how individual decisions affect the overall project.

A successful general contractor is more than a builder. They are a planner, coordinator, communicator, problem solver, and project manager whose responsibility is to bring together dozens of moving parts into one successful renovation.

Responsibilities of a General Contractor

  • Project planning and scheduling
  • Budget development and cost management
  • Hiring and coordinating subcontractors
  • Material procurement and logistics
  • Permits and inspection coordination
  • Quality control
  • Jobsite safety
  • Communication with homeowners and design professionals
  • Managing unforeseen conditions and change orders
  • Project closeout and punch list coordination

Common Misconceptions

  • The contractor just hires subcontractors. Coordinating trades is only one responsibility among many that keep a project moving.
  • The contractor is on-site performing every task. Most general contractors manage the project while specialty trades perform the work within their areas of expertise.
  • The contractor controls everything. Many decisions depend on homeowner approvals, design professionals, inspectors, suppliers, and local agencies.
  • If something changes, the contractor must have made a mistake. Many changes result from homeowner decisions, hidden conditions, product availability, or discoveries made during demolition.
  • Project management shouldn't cost very much. Planning, coordination, communication, scheduling, documentation, and risk management are significant parts of the value a contractor provides.

Foundation Tip

When evaluating contractors, look beyond craftsmanship alone. Strong planning, communication, organization, and project management often have just as much influence on a successful renovation.

Closing Thought

The visible work of a renovation is only part of the project. Behind every successful remodel is a great deal of planning, coordination, and decision-making that homeowners may never see—but benefit from every day.
