When Should I Hire a General Contractor?

Decide whether contractor input is most useful during early planning and design or after the construction documents are sufficiently complete.

By Jon Lourash · Foundation Renovation Coaching

Published September 21, 2026

Many homeowners assume the general contractor is the last professional they hire, after the design is complete and permits are ready. While that is one valid approach, it is not the only one. Depending on the project, involving a contractor earlier can provide valuable insight during planning and design.

For smaller or straightforward renovations, it may make sense to complete the design first and then invite contractors to submit proposals. This allows you to compare bids based on the same scope of work and select the contractor who is the best fit.

For larger or more complex projects, bringing a contractor into the process earlier can help identify constructability concerns, develop realistic budgets, review schedules, and provide input on material availability. Early collaboration may reduce redesign and help keep the project aligned with your goals.

Neither approach guarantees a better outcome. The right timing depends on the complexity of your renovation, the professionals already involved, and how you prefer to make decisions. Some homeowners value competitive bidding, while others value continuous collaboration throughout the design process.

Regardless of when you hire a contractor, remember that the contractor is more than the person who builds the project. A good general contractor coordinates trades, manages schedules, oversees quality, communicates with the project team, and helps solve problems as they arise.

You May Benefit from Hiring a Contractor Early If...

  • Your project involves structural changes or major additions.
  • You want budgeting input while the design is still evolving.
  • You are considering a Design-Build approach.
  • You want advice on constructability or phasing.
  • You value ongoing collaboration during planning.

Waiting Until Design Is Complete May Make Sense If...

  • You want to compare multiple contractor proposals.
  • You already have a completed set of construction drawings.
  • You prefer to separate design from construction.
  • Your project scope is well-defined before bidding.

Common Misconceptions

  • The contractor should always be hired last. Many successful projects benefit from contractor input during planning and design.
  • Hiring a contractor early means you can't compare prices. Some delivery methods allow early collaboration while still discussing pricing transparently; other projects intentionally use competitive bidding after design.
  • A general contractor only manages subcontractors. General contractors also coordinate schedules, permits, communication, quality, and problem-solving.
  • The contractor is responsible for every design decision. Design responsibilities vary depending on the project team and delivery method.

Foundation Tip

Think about when you need a contractor's expertise—not just when you need construction to begin. The right timing depends on your project and the level of collaboration you want.

Closing Thought

Hiring a general contractor is not simply the start of construction. It is the beginning of a working relationship that can influence planning, communication, budgeting, and the overall success of your renovation.
