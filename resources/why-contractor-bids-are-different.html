Understanding Bid Differences

Why Are Contractor Bids So Different?

Learn how scope interpretations, allowances, exclusions, supervision, overhead, sequencing and risk assumptions create different proposal totals.

By Jon Lourash · Foundation Renovation Coaching

Published September 21, 2026

In This Guide

Many homeowners are surprised when contractor proposals for the same project differ by thousands—or even tens of thousands—of dollars. It's natural to wonder whether one contractor is overpriced or another has made a mistake. In reality, bid differences are often the result of different assumptions, business models, and approaches to managing the project.

A contractor's proposal reflects more than the cost of labor and materials. It also reflects how the contractor interprets the scope of work, manages risk, plans the project, and delivers their services.

One of the most common reasons bids vary is scope. Even when contractors receive the same drawings, they may make different assumptions about what is included. One proposal may include permits, demolition, temporary protection, finish details, or project management that another contractor has excluded.

Allowances can also create large pricing differences. One contractor may include realistic allowances for cabinetry, flooring, lighting, or plumbing fixtures, while another may use lower placeholder amounts that reduce the initial proposal but increase costs later when selections are made.

Every contractor also has different operating costs. Company size, staffing, insurance, supervision, scheduling systems, warranty support, and project management all influence pricing. A higher proposal does not automatically mean a contractor is overpriced, just as a lower proposal does not necessarily represent better value.

Finally, contractors have different levels of risk tolerance. Some include additional costs to account for uncertainty, while others assume fewer risks or rely on change orders if conditions differ from expectations.

Reasons Contractor Bids May Differ

  • Different interpretations of the project scope
  • Different allowances for unfinished selections
  • Different exclusions
  • Different project management and supervision approaches
  • Different subcontractor and supplier pricing
  • Different overhead and operating costs
  • Different schedules or construction sequencing
  • Different approaches to managing project risk

Common Misconceptions

  • The highest bid is a rip-off. A higher proposal may include additional scope, stronger project management, higher-quality materials, or more realistic allowances.
  • The lowest bid saves the most money. Lower bids sometimes exclude work or rely on allowances that increase costs later.
  • Every contractor is pricing exactly the same project. Even with identical plans, contractors often make different assumptions about the work.
  • Contractors should all arrive at the same number. Construction estimating involves judgment as well as pricing. Different companies have different costs and processes.
  • Large price differences mean someone made a mistake. Sometimes they do, but more often the proposals reflect different approaches to delivering the project.

Foundation Tip

If one proposal stands out as significantly higher or lower than the others, ask the contractor to walk you through the scope, assumptions, allowances, and exclusions. The conversation often reveals more than the price itself.

Closing Thought

A contractor's proposal tells a story about how they intend to complete your renovation. Understanding why bids differ allows you to compare proposals more confidently and choose the contractor who offers the best overall fit for your project—not simply the lowest price.
