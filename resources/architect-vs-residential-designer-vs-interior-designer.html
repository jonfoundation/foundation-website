Choosing Design Professionals

Should I Hire an Architect, a Residential Designer, or an Interior Designer?

Understand the distinct roles of architects, residential designers and interior designers—and when a renovation may benefit from more than one.

By Jon Lourash · Foundation Renovation Coaching

Published September 21, 2026

One of the first questions homeowners face after deciding to renovate is who should help design the project. Architects, residential designers, and interior designers all contribute valuable expertise, but their responsibilities, training, and areas of focus are different. The best choice depends on the scope and complexity of your renovation.

An architect is typically involved when a project includes significant structural changes, complex additions, unique site conditions, or other design challenges. Architects are trained to balance aesthetics, functionality, building codes, and technical requirements while coordinating with engineers and other consultants when necessary.

A residential designer focuses specifically on homes. Many residential designers create construction drawings for remodels, additions, and new homes while working closely with homeowners to develop practical layouts and functional living spaces. Depending on local regulations, a residential designer may be able to prepare everything needed for many residential renovation projects.

Interior designers focus on how a space looks, feels, and functions once it is built. Their work may include space planning, cabinetry layouts, lighting, finish selections, furniture, color palettes, and creating a cohesive design throughout the home. Some interior designers also prepare construction documents for interior renovations, while others primarily concentrate on the finished environment.

These professions are not competitors. In many successful renovations they work together. An architect may design the overall structure, a residential designer may develop detailed layouts for a remodel, and an interior designer may refine the finishes and interior experience. The right combination depends entirely on the goals of the project.

Rather than asking which professional is 'best,' ask which professional—or combination of professionals—has the expertise your project requires. The answer may be different for a cosmetic kitchen update than it is for a whole-home renovation or a second-story addition.

Comparing Design Professionals

  • Architect: Complex projects, structural changes, additions, custom homes, code coordination.
  • Residential Designer: Residential remodels, additions, functional layouts, construction drawings for many home projects.
  • Interior Designer: Interior layouts, finishes, lighting, cabinetry, furnishings, and overall aesthetic experience.

Questions to Ask Yourself

  • Will my renovation involve structural changes or a major addition?
  • Am I primarily changing the layout, the appearance, or both?
  • Do I need help selecting finishes and furnishings?
  • Will multiple professionals need to coordinate during design?
  • What level of design support will help me feel confident before construction begins?

Foundation Tip

Every renovation deserves the right level of design expertise. Matching the professional to the project often leads to better planning, clearer expectations, and a smoother construction process.

Closing Thought

A successful design is rarely the result of one person working alone. Whether you work with an architect, a residential designer, an interior designer, or a combination of professionals, the goal is the same: creating a home that functions well and reflects the way you want to live.
