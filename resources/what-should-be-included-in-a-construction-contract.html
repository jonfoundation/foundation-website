Construction Contract Essentials

What Should Be Included in a Construction Contract?

Understand the scope, price, payment, schedule, change, insurance, warranty and closeout terms that should be clear before signing.

By Jon Lourash · Foundation Renovation Coaching

Published September 21, 2026

In This Guide

A construction contract is more than a document that lists a price. It establishes the expectations, responsibilities, and procedures that will guide the renovation from start to finish. A well-written contract helps both the homeowner and the contractor understand what is included, how decisions will be handled, and how unexpected situations will be addressed.

Every project is different, but most construction contracts contain several core elements. Understanding these sections allows homeowners to review proposals more confidently and ask better questions before work begins.

The contract should clearly define the scope of work. This section describes what the contractor is expected to build and often references drawings, specifications, or other project documents. The more clearly the scope is defined, the less room there is for misunderstandings later.

It should also explain the project price and payment schedule. Whether the project uses a Fixed Price or Cost Plus contract, homeowners should understand how payments are calculated, when they are due, and what milestones or conditions trigger each payment.

Another important section describes how changes will be handled. Renovations often involve owner-requested changes or unforeseen conditions. A clear change order process helps everyone understand how additional work will be approved, priced, and documented before it proceeds.

Finally, the contract should explain practical topics such as permits, scheduling, warranty responsibilities, insurance, dispute resolution, and project closeout. These details help establish expectations long before construction begins.

Key Elements of a Construction Contract

  • Scope of work
  • Drawings and specifications
  • Contract price and payment schedule
  • Allowances and exclusions
  • Change order process
  • Project schedule or estimated duration
  • Permits and inspection responsibilities
  • Insurance requirements
  • Warranty information
  • Procedures for project completion and final payment

Common Misconceptions

  • A signed contract guarantees there will never be disagreements. A good contract reduces misunderstandings by clearly defining expectations, but communication remains essential throughout the project.
  • The contract only protects the contractor. A well-written contract protects both parties by documenting responsibilities, expectations, and procedures.
  • If something isn't written down, everyone will remember what was discussed. Important decisions should be documented so there is a shared understanding later.
  • The price is the most important part of the contract. The scope of work, payment terms, schedule, and change order process are just as important as the total cost.
  • Construction contracts are only legal paperwork. A contract is also a project management tool that helps everyone understand how the renovation will be delivered.

Foundation Tip

Before signing, ask yourself whether you understand how the project will be managed—not just how much it will cost. If any section of the contract is unclear, ask questions before construction begins.

Closing Thought

A construction contract should create clarity, not confusion. The more clearly it defines the project, the easier it becomes for both the homeowner and the contractor to work together toward a successful renovation.
