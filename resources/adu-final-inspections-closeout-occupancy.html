ADU Final Approval & Closeout

ADU Final Inspections, Closeout and Occupancy

Agency approval, contractor closeout and the homeowner’s final walk are related—but they are not the same checkpoint.

By Jon Lourash · Foundation Renovation Coaching

Published October 6, 2026

Final approval is a process, not simply a last walk-through. The specific inspections and occupancy terminology vary by jurisdiction, but the project should not be treated as complete until required approvals, corrections, utility coordination and closeout documents are resolved.

Confirm Required Final Inspections

The permitting authority determines which finals are required. The contractor should coordinate outstanding building, electrical, plumbing, mechanical, energy or other inspections and track correction items to completion.

Resolve Corrections Before Closeout

A failed or incomplete final inspection is not unusual. The important point is to document the correction, assign responsibility, complete the work and schedule any needed reinspection. Do not assume the ADU may be occupied before the applicable approvals are in place.

Collect the Closeout Package

Ask for permits and final records, inspection signoffs, equipment manuals, warranties, product information, keys, remotes, controls, maintenance instructions and any required testing or commissioning documentation.

Complete a Final Owner Walk

Use the approved scope and selections as the checklist. Review finishes, doors, windows, fixtures, appliances, drainage, exterior access, equipment operation and any remaining punch-list items. Record items in writing with a clear path to completion.

Foundation Tip

Keep the final approval and closeout documents together in one durable folder. They are useful for maintenance, future repairs, insurance and a later sale.

How Foundation Can Help

Homeowners who want independent decision support at closeout can use the Foundation Final Walk™. The on-site consultation helps document visible punch items, incomplete work, warranty questions and other closeout items to discuss with the contractor before final payment. It does not replace required inspections, professional testing, commissioning or agency approval.

Important Disclaimer

This article provides general planning information, not permitting, inspection, legal or contractor advice. Verify the required approvals and occupancy rules with the applicable agency and project team.
