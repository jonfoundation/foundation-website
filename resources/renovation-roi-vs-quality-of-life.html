Renovation Value Decisions

Return on Investment vs. Quality of Life

Balance resale value with comfort, function, accessibility, enjoyment and the length of time you expect to remain in your home.

By Jon Lourash · Foundation Renovation Coaching

Published September 21, 2026

Homeowners often ask whether a renovation is “worth the money.” While return on investment is important, it is only one way to measure success. Some projects increase resale value, while others primarily improve the way you live in your home. Both outcomes can represent a worthwhile investment.

Return on investment compares renovation cost with the value it may add when the home is sold. Published averages vary by location, market conditions, the age of the home and the quality of the work. They should be treated as general guidance rather than guarantees.

Quality of life considers how a renovation improves daily experience. Better function, storage, natural light, accessibility, energy efficiency and enjoyment can provide meaningful value that may not appear in an appraisal.

The right balance depends on your goals. If you expect to sell soon, market appeal may deserve more attention. If this is your long-term home, comfort, function and enjoyment may carry more weight. Addressing deferred maintenance also protects the home and future renovation investments.

Projects Often Chosen for Quality of Life

  • Improving the layout of frequently used spaces
  • Creating better storage and organization
  • Accessibility and aging-in-place modifications
  • Outdoor living areas
  • Energy efficiency and indoor comfort

Projects Often Evaluated for ROI

  • Kitchen updates
  • Bathroom renovations
  • Curb appeal improvements
  • Exterior replacements such as siding or windows
  • Addressing deferred maintenance before selling

Questions to Ask Yourself

  • How long do I expect to stay in this home?
  • Will this renovation solve a daily frustration?
  • Am I renovating for resale, personal enjoyment or both?
  • Does this investment protect the long-term condition of my home?
  • Would I regret not making this improvement if I remain here for several more years?

Foundation Tip

The best renovation is not always the one with the highest resale value. It is the one that supports your goals, lifestyle and the future you envision for your home.

Closing Thought

Financial return is important, but it is only one measure of success. A thoughtful renovation should improve the value of your home while also improving the way you live in it.
